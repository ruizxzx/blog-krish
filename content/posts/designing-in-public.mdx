I used to sit on projects until they felt "done" before showing anyone. That habit was mostly fear wearing a productivity costume.

## What changed

Posting early drafts — genuinely rough ones — forced two things:

1. I had to articulate what a piece of work was *for*, not just what it looked like.
2. Feedback arrived while a decision was still cheap to reverse.

## A small example

A layout I shipped last month went through three completely different grid structures before landing anywhere close to final. Only one of those iterations ever got polished. The other two existed for a day, got torn apart in a comment thread, and were gone.

> The version you're proud enough to publish first is rarely the version that ships.

## The discomfort is the signal

If sharing a draft doesn't feel slightly exposing, it's probably not early enough to be useful feedback. That's counterintuitive, but it's held up every time I've tested it.
