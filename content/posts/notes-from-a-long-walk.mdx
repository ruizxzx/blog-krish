I set out with no route planned, which is rarer than it should be. Most walks I take have a destination baked in — a shop, a train station, a loop I've done a hundred times.

## What no destination does

Without a destination, there's no pace to keep. That changes what your attention does. Mine wandered into a problem I'd been stuck on for a week, and untangled about a third of it before I'd noticed I was thinking about it at all.

## Three unrelated things I noticed

- A building under renovation had kept its original brick facade, scaffolded in front of an entirely new structure
- Nobody was on their phone at a bus stop that usually has six people staring down
- The same street sounded completely different without the usual traffic

None of that is profound. It's just what happens when you're not trying to get anywhere.
