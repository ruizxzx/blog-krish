For a while, my morning routine was a checklist: wake at a fixed time, cold shower, journal three pages, review the day's priorities, then start work — all before a set deadline. It worked, in the sense that I did all the things.

## What optimizing actually cost

Every morning became a performance of a morning, rather than a morning. I wasn't awake yet when I started demanding output from myself.

## The experiment

For a month I tried the opposite: no checklist, no fixed wake time, coffee made slowly and drunk while looking out a window instead of at a phone. Work started whenever it started.

Some mornings that meant I was "productive" by 7am. Others, not until 10. The variance stopped bothering me faster than I expected.

## Where I landed

Not fully unstructured, not fully optimized. A short, non-negotiable list of two things (make the bed, make coffee properly) and then genuine slack. The rest of the day tends to sort itself out.
